# Goal fixed, plan flexible

> Source: quotations from The Scrum Guide 2020 (Ken Schwaber & Jeff Sutherland), marked where they appear.
> License: the quotations are CC BY-SA 4.0 (© 2020 Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland).

What stays fixed in a project, what may change, and how each log writes its commitment. Read this file when you write or change a Goal, the Definition of Done or a commitment table, or when someone wants to change what a log committed to. The list lines that point here are `goal-fixed` and `commitment-table`; `po-owns-commitments` stands in the list alone.

## What is fixed and what may change (`goal-fixed`)

Each log has one fixed commitment: the Product Goal, the Sprint Goal, the design goal; the Definition of Done is not weakened either. Everything under a commitment — items, refinement points, acceptance criteria, the How, the chosen design solution — may change as you learn, and each log records what changed and why. Only the Product Owner changes a commitment, and only the Product Owner cancels a Sprint whose goal can no longer be met (`po-owns-commitments`).

What each log adds:

- **Sprint:** acceptance criteria and refinement points may be renegotiated with the Product Owner, or with the Scrum Master within a written handover; write each change and its reason in chapter 4, Increment notes.
- **Design:** sharpen the design goal during Empathize; once it is set, only the Product Owner changes it. The questions, the map and the solution may change with what the work shows; write what changed and why in the chapter it belongs to.
- **Product log:** where an item's change and a Product Goal change are written is `product-log-changes`.

## The commitment table (`commitment-table`)

Keep the product statement in the product log's section 1.1, the Product Goal in its section 1.2, the Sprint Goal in the sprint log's section 1.1, and the design goal in the design log's section 1.2. Each has one one-row table: the first cell is `Commitment` (`承诺` in a Chinese log), the second is the exact text, without bold.

- Keep one copy, not another at the file's top; its section heading identifies it.
- The table is the only source of the current commitment.
- Optional reasons can follow below it; they are not part of the commitment or what a reader extracts from the table.
- Exact text on a stage decision line is a snapshot of the commitment at that time, not a second source; do not update it when the current goal changes.
- Give each goal one short name and keep using that name.
- An IDEO log has no commitment of its own: its For field points to the log it serves.

The commitments hook, the board and the check script read this table, so keep its shape exactly.

## What the Scrum Guide says

The Scrum Guide says it this way. On the Product Goal: "They must fulfill (or abandon) one objective before taking on the next." During the Sprint: "No changes are made that would endanger the Sprint Goal", "Quality does not decrease", and "Scope may be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner as more is learned." And: "Only the Product Owner has the authority to cancel the Sprint."
